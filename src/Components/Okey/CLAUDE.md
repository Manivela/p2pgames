# Okey

How each part works is in its code comments; this file holds the conventions, decisions and gotchas they don't.

## Rules and state

- Rules are pure functions, `(state, player, ...args) => newState | "toast message" | undefined` (no change), each with tests: every game is played by `americano.js`, in rounds, wrapping the moves of a hand in `game.js`, meld and finish checks in `melds.js` / `checkFinish.js`. `OkeyContext.jsx` only wires them to Yjs.
- A ruleset's numbers and switches are stored in the game as `rules` and read from there, so a game keeps the rules it started with, but for what the table changes mid-game in the ⋯ menu's Rules (`TableSettings`, anyone's to change, said on the table): every setting but the rounds and "The table"'s group, which the deal and the seats are laid out by. A rule reads its setting at the move, so a change counts from then on. The presets (classic, Americano, 101) are in `rulesets.js`, each a list of rounds that open their own way (`melds`, a points `opening`, or neither: from the hand) and `DEFAULTS` with the settings it changes, so every ruleset has every setting and nothing reads one with a fallback. Older builds' games aren't kept playable: a save from before rounds doesn't load (`decodeSave`), and a kept ruleset gets the settings it lacks from `DEFAULTS`. 101 follows the Eşli 101 Turnuvası's rules (the PDF linked in `rulesets.js`) over pagat.com's, each rule its own switch. Partners (eşli) and katlamalı (`raiseOpening`: an opening with points beats the highest so far that hand; the PDF doesn't have it) are the table's to tick in the lobby's settings (⚙); the PDF's score multipliers are `doubling`, a different thing. The gösterge scores nothing in any game: 101 as the PDF has it (a -101 for showing its twin was invented, taken out), and classic too, though many tables play gösterme (the user's call); it's never a pair either. Once someone opens with pairs everyone can look through the discards (`peekWithPairs`), the user's call: the PDF lets only the pairs opener. An işler taş can be caught until the next discard, as in Americano (`catchUntilDraw` off; the PDF ends it at the next draw), and stays free to take once caught (`takeCaught`), unlike Americano: both the user's call. Not yet: "devam" (×4), best-of-three matches.
- A rule that differs between games is a setting in the ruleset, never a check of which game it is (a preset's id) or of what the whole game opens with: players make their own games by combining settings, and the presets are just their values. What a round shows or allows goes by that round (`roundOf`, `isElden`).
- The lobby's settings are `setup`, the ruleset the next game starts with: shared, anyone's to change, kept by New Game (`GameSettings.jsx`). A new setting gets a key in `DEFAULTS` and a line in that file's groups, saying in full what it does (no tooltips: the text is the explanation) with its Turkish, a setting that hangs on a switch right under it, one the deal or the seats are laid out by in "The table" (out of mid-game changes); and a check in `invalid` when a bad value would break the rules at every table playing it.
- Tile id `${color}${rank}-${copy}` (`red7-0`, fake okey `yellow👌-1`); a ruleset's `tileSets` (2 unless it says, up to 4) makes copies 0 to 3, and `seats` goes up to 8, the seats past the fourth showing one at a time as the table fills (`tableSeats`). `okeyTile` is the indicator; the okey is the next rank in its colour. A hand has two rack rows of `rowOf(state, player)` slots: the ruleset's `rowSlots` (13; 101's 15), or the player's own, set any time in the Rules (`setRow`, its own sync key: the tiles' size follows it), so anything reading a hand's rows asks for its player's; `null` is a gap, and gaps separate groups. The hex in a tile is game state (saves, `melds.js` compares it): colour tiles through their `c-<colour>` class.
- A hand ends when someone throws their last tile to the middle (`finishGame`; discarding it is refused), where it lies as `finishTile` until the next deal, or when the pile runs out: the round ends (`pileOutCounts`: scored, or dealt again), or with `reshuffle` (classic) the player whose turn it is shuffles the discards into a new pile or ends the round (`pileOut`, `endPile`).
- A move the round end should show is logged with `logMove` (`turnMoves`, emptied as the turn passes).
- Sync (`sync.js`) splits the state into Yjs keys by writer, so simultaneous moves by different players both survive. New top-level state lands in the `turn` key, written by the player whose turn it is; state another player can write at the same moment gets a key of its own. A hand is written only by its owner: tiles given to someone go through their `inbox`. Whole-game changes (start, new game, reset, load) go through `replaceState`.
- A browser is one player (`useStore.js`, localStorage): its tabs are the same player. Joining a table, a page sits down in its first empty seat (only once the table has reached it: an empty one of its own would be written over everyone's), but not again once it stood up or was freed. A player who drops out (a reload, a phone that slept) leaves their seat `away: <user id>`, and only they sit back down in it by themselves, a bot playing it meanwhile making way. A round deals only seats sat in or `away` (`nextRound`): a seat stood up from (`standUp`, its hand left in the round for a friend) or freed (`freeSeat`, out of the round at once) has neither, so it stays out, however `allSeats` rebuilds it. Mid-round an empty seat at the table can be sat in, by anyone or a bot (`sit`): dealt a hand from the middle pile while it holds one (`dealsIn`; never with partners), scoring level with whoever's losing; a freed seat's hand, discards and inbox go back into the pile. Either change to who's in the round replaces the table (`write` in `OkeyContext.jsx`), as a deal does. Every seat change is a rule (`sit`, `markGone`, `seatToSitBack`, `seatToJoin`, `rename`, `standUp`, `freeSeat`); `OkeyContext.jsx` only feeds them who's present (awareness). A seat dropped out of shows its player's name marked away (`awayName`).
- New versions (`src/version.js`): each page tells the table its build and the deal it has (awareness). A page older than the site or than someone at the table offers it in the ⋯ menu (an orange dot on ⋯, easy to ignore), and reloads by itself between hands only when a page at the table runs the newer build and has our deal: it keeps the game meanwhile, and one just come in may not have it yet. Never auto-reload on the site's version alone, or the whole table reloads at once and the game is gone.
- Rules and shared messages are English, shown through `t()` (`src/i18n.js`): every new string gets a Turkish line. `TR` takes the first match, so a specific pattern sits above any catch-all it would hit (`No tiles left` above `(.+) left`). `i18n.test.js` checks the `return "…"` messages of the rules files it lists: a new rules file joins the list.

## Table

- Show what everyone at a real table sees and leave the thinking to the player: no hints about what to lay, no auto-sort. Make hands faster, not smarter. The one exception, asked for: El aç's first press picks what the rules would lay down (the opening they find, then the sets and runs as they lie on the rack; never pairs, those are picked on purpose), for the player to change or lay.
- On the pair round (Americano's 4 çift), anyone can look through the discard piles, the user's house rule (`peekPairRounds`): a tap spreads a pile out (`DiscardPile`).
- Gestures over buttons wherever one fits. Moves that end or replace the game live in the ⋯ menu (`TableMenu.jsx`) behind a second tap (`Confirm`).
- The turn's ring (a bar over your own rack) fills once in the time the player waits before they may discard (`discardWait`: the ruleset's `claimWaitSeconds` while someone could take the discard), full at once when they don't wait. Nothing times out: the turn waits for the player.
- One layout for every screen (`Board.jsx`, a CSS grid), sized in tile widths `--t` (`useTileSize`); only the size changes. Anything that comes and goes (badges, Sit, the tea glass, Catch) is placed absolutely, so its row never shifts; along the top it stays inside the row, as a full table's melds come right up to it.
- Phones play everything a hand needs; extras don't all have to fit one. Something a phone has no room for (an editor, a long list of settings) can be left off phones, as long as nothing needed to play is. When the same layout fits a phone with little work, keep it the same instead of leaving the phone out.
- Bootstrap 3's CSS is global: give table parts names of their own (`.half`, `.seat`), clear of its classes (`.row`, `.hidden`, `.label`, `.open`, `.fade`, `.table`…).
- A face-down tile renders only `.back`, its colour and number out of the page: inspecting the pile would show the next tile.
- No `useDragLayer`: its listener goes stale between a render and its effects, so a drag ending then (a quick flick on a phone) leaves the preview in the air. The preview (`index.jsx`) reads the monitor through `useSyncExternalStore`; what a drop target needs of the held tile comes from its own `useDrop` collect (`Slot.jsx`), and the rack keeps every slot that says it's held over, as react-dnd can say two at once (`Rack.jsx`).
- Animations (`moves.js`) find tiles and places by `data-tile-id`, `data-seat`, `data-pile`, `data-meld`, `data-hand`: a new place a tile can be needs its attribute; a picture of a tile (`TileFace`) carries none, so nothing flies to it. Flights measure screen rects, which Chrome and WebKit both report right inside `zoom` (small piles, melds, shown hands).

## Verify

1. Rules: `npx vitest run` from the repo root (from this folder it skips test files). Deal a known position with `seated(n)` (`game.test.js`) and `game.startGame(state, { okeyTile, hands })`, or `americano.startGame(state, deal, rules)`. Americano's claim wait: `drawTile` / `discardTile` take an optional `now`, and `drawTile` a `turnStart` the wait runs from when it's earlier; tests discard at `afterWait(s)`, scripts pass `Infinity`.
2. `npm run lint`: no errors (prettier runs inside it).
3. End to end: `npm run test:e2e` runs `e2e/okey.e2e.js` in Playwright's Linux image (`e2e/docker.sh`; the first run pulls ~1 GB), so screenshots match on every machine; `npx playwright test` on the Mac runs the same tests without the screenshot comparisons. Positions come from `e2e/table.js`: the real rules on a fixed deal, written with `window.okey`. A new table stage gets the fit check (`check()`: nothing overlaps or falls off-screen) and a reference screenshot. After an intended visual change: `npm run test:e2e -- --update-snapshots`, look at each new PNG in `e2e/okey.e2e.js-snapshots/`, commit them (a change within the per-pixel tolerance keeps the old image; `=all` rewrites every one).
4. A layout change, by eye: 1348×870, 1024×768, 820×1180, 844×390 and 667×320, each in the lobby, mid-game, at round end and watching without a seat, in English and Turkish.

Done when vitest, lint and the Docker e2e are green and every layout change has been seen at each size and stage, its strings in Turkish too.

Driving the table (Playwright MCP or a script, against the dev server):

- Another player is another browser: `browser.newContext()`, synced over the network in about a second; what its page does before the game arrives is lost (`arrived()` in the e2e waits). Remove localStorage `user` before a page first loads to be asked a name, and set sessionStorage `watch` to keep it from sitting down by itself (`sitDown()`). A tab reloaded while alone in its room comes back to an empty table: the game lives only in the open pages.
- A bot is a seat whose `user.bot` is set, played by the page whose `userKey` it holds (`bot.js`); the e2e's `seatBots` users aren't bots and stay unplayed. sessionStorage `botPace` (ms between moves) runs them fast.
- A tab plays a newer build in development with `sessionStorage.setItem("build", n)` before it loads (`secondPlayer()` in the e2e).
- Dev-only `window.okey` reads and writes the shared state; to play any seat: `g = await import("/src/Components/Okey/game.js")`, then `okey.set(g.drawTile(okey.get(), seat, tile, slot))`. Dev-only `window.okeyFlights` counts animation flights.
- `dragTo` works with react-dnd once source and target carry a `data-*` to find them by. Your own rack is the only `.rack`.
- Turkish: override `navigator.language` with `addInitScript` before loading (CDP's locale override leaves it alone).
- Touch: react-dnd's touch backend drags the mouse too, on every screen (never the browser's own drag and drop, which goes through the OS: players on Windows 11 lost copy and paste in every app while at the table). For real touches (a tile's hold, a finger's box) call CDP `Emulation.setTouchEmulationEnabled` before navigating, then `Input.dispatchTouchEvent` start / move / end.
- Report a problem (Sentry's form) loads only on the deployed site: serve a production build at the live address (`route`) and intercept `ingest.us.sentry.io`.
